02 / Vibe manufacturing

Intent-led, agent-assisted creation of physical products.

A definition worth keeping — and a flow you can actually walk.

  1. 01ImagineA sentence, a sketch, a photo or a STEP file.
  2. 02SpecifyIntent becomes requirements and acceptance tests.
  3. 03SourceBuild around parts that exist, in stock, today.
  4. 04DesignParametric CAD and firmware an agent can edit.
  5. 05MakeSlice, print, assemble. Your first factory can be local.
  6. 06TestForm, fit, function — and five honest people.
  7. 07ScalePilot batch, design for manufacture, production.

Agents are great at

  • Turning intent into requirements
  • Comparing datasheets and options
  • Drafting firmware and CAD code
  • Writing test plans and checklists
  • Keeping decisions documented

Only the real world can

  • Enforce physics and tolerances
  • Confirm real stock and price
  • Tell you how a click feels
  • Prove that it's safe

The real world still gets the final vote — so every project on MyriadFlow states what is verified, what is estimated, and what still needs testing.

04 / TVASTR · manufacturing intelligence

The engine that turns a brief into a build.

Named for the Vedic maker and fashioner of forms, TVASTR turns a brief into structured specifications, parametric design, feasibility checks, prototype jobs, test reports and a production handoff. Every physical test leaves a report the next revision can use.

One narrow loop, run honestly

brief → PartSpec → parametric CAD → DFM checks → manufacturing plan → slice → print → PrintReport → revision.

import cadquery as cq
D, H, WALL = 72, 28, 2.4  # mm
shell = (
  cq.Workplane("XY")
  .polygon(6, D)   # hex footprint
  .extrude(H)
  .edges(">Z").fillet(4)
  .faces(">Z").shell(-WALL)
)
wall 2.4 mmwall 2.0 mm ✓ DFM · min wall 1.2 mm

CAD as code

Not a render — a parametric model with dimensions, constraints and history. Change one number, regenerate, re-check, export STEP, STL or 3MF.

TVASTR can

  • Ask clarifying questions
  • Draft a spec
  • Generate parametric parts
  • Check geometry and materials
  • Prepare a print job
  • Observe results and propose revisions

You approve

  • Designs
  • Spending
  • Machine starts
  • Production commitments
  • Campaign terms
  • Major changes

Beyond a print. As projects mature, the right path may be CNC, sheet metal, electronics assembly, injection moulding, textiles or a manufacturing partner. Printers and suppliers plug in as structured capabilities; price, quality and schedule come from real quotes and tests.

Funding is a launch tool, not a default.

Preorders, reservations, rewards or community backing — chosen per project. Before a campaign opens, the creator states the minimum viable batch, per-unit cost, tooling and test needs, contingency and timing. If a goal is missed or a prototype fails, the page says what happens next.

The flywheel.

More ideas bring more builders. Visible prototypes bring better feedback. Better evidence helps supporters judge a launch. Finished products build trust — and reusable manufacturing knowledge for the next wave of creators.

07 / Made to your taste

A world of products made for one person at a time.

For a century, factories decided what we could own: one design, a million copies, close enough for most. Vibe manufacturing flips the order. A product can start from your hands, your desk, your colour and your problem — and still be made properly.

  • Your fit. Sized to the hand, the shelf, the body that uses it.
  • Your material. Matte, soft, tough or glowing — the right material in the right place.
  • Your run. One prototype, a pilot batch, or a product for everyone who wanted what you wanted.

09 / Execution

Start with one reliable flow.

The destination is a launchpad for hardware ideas of every kind. The first build proves one complete path through the hard middle: turning an ambiguous request into a physical test and an honest public project.

  1. ACompile

    PartSpec → valid parametric enclosure → STEP/STL preview.

    Dimensions and features survive revisions.
  2. BMake

    Manufacturing plan, DFM, slicing, a reference printer connection.

    A real enclosure prints with the right material and profile.
  3. CLearn

    Job status, PrintReport, test notes, guided redesign.

    Failure or feedback leads to a traceable, improved part.
  4. DShare

    Creator workspace, project page, waitlist, updates and feedback.

    A visitor understands the proof and what remains.
  5. ELaunch

    Campaign configuration, milestones, supporter tracking.

    A vetted pilot run with explicit terms and progress.
  6. FScale

    Supplier handoff, production QA, orders and fulfilment.

    A completed batch reaches buyers with visible status.

Early boundaries. Prove an enclosure before arbitrary assemblies. Production electronics, certification, tooling and high-volume manufacturing go through specialist review and partner processes. A first print is never presented as proof that a device is safe, certified or production-ready.
